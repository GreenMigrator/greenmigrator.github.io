About Ramadan & Eid

Ramadan is the holiest month in Islam, observed by millions of Muslims around the world. It is a time of fasting, prayer, reflection, and community. During Ramadan, Muslims fast from dawn to sunset, refraining from food, drink, and other physical needs to purify the soul and grow closer to God.

Fasting is one of the Five Pillars of Islam, and it is meant to encourage self-discipline, gratitude, and compassion for those who are less fortunate. The fast is broken each evening with a meal called Iftar, which is often shared with family and friends.

At the end of Ramadan, Muslims celebrate Eid al-Fitr, a joyous holiday that marks the conclusion of the fasting period. Eid begins with a special prayer at the mosque, followed by feasting, giving gifts, and acts of charity. It is a time of unity, gratitude, and spreading happiness among loved ones.
